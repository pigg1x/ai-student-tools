AI Assistants

General-purpose AI chatbots for anything college throws at you

View all 6 tools →

ChatGPT

Freemium
Everything

ChatGPT is the first tab I open at 2am before an essay is due. The free tier got me through freshman year - explaining concepts I zoned out on, outlining essays, debugging code that made no sense. One warning I learned the hard way: it will absolutely invent sources if you let it. I never paste its citations into a paper without checking them myself now.

Claude

Freemium
Long readings & essays

The reason I switched from ChatGPT for research papers is the context window - I can drop an entire 200-page PDF in and ask questions like 'what does the author argue in chapter 3?' No more copy-pasting chunks of reading. For essay-heavy majors the free tier is honestly generous enough to be your main AI.

Google Gemini

Freemium
Google Docs users

If your whole academic life runs on Google - Docs, Classroom, Gmail - Gemini just works. It reads your docs without you exporting anything, and it's genuinely good at explaining diagrams and charts from lecture slides. It's not the flashiest AI, but it's the least friction when everything you own is already in Google.

Microsoft Copilot

Free
Free GPT-4-level answers

Copilot is the cheat code most students don't know about: a free Microsoft account gets you GPT-4-level answers, image generation, and web search with citations - things ChatGPT gates behind $20/month. I use it as my 'free premium AI' for quick research questions. The catch: it forgets context faster, so long conversations get frustrating.

DeepSeek

Free
Math and reasoning, free

DeepSeek went viral in my dorm for one reason: it's completely free and weirdly good at math. I threw calc problem sets at it that made other free chatbots cry and it worked through them step by step. The English is slightly stiffer than ChatGPT, and I double-check anything factual, but for a broke student who needs a reasoning engine at 2am, it's hard to beat.

Poe

Freemium
Testing every AI model in one app

Poe is one app where I can bounce the same question between ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and a bunch of niche bots to compare answers. When one model gives me a lazy essay outline, I paste it into another and say 'do better.' Free daily credits run out fast though, so I save it for when the free tiers of individual apps annoy me.

Essay & Writing

Draft, paraphrase, polish and grammar-check your assignments

View all 7 tools →

Grammarly

Freemium
Final essay polish

I installed the browser extension freshman week and forgot about it until I wrote my first essay without it. It catches typos, passive voice, and tone issues everywhere - docs, emails, even messages to professors. I only paid for Premium during thesis season, and the student discount makes it like 40% cheaper. Worth every cent for that one month.

QuillBot

Freemium
Paraphrasing

My go-to for the classic problem: I know what I want to say but it comes out clunky. Paste it in, get a cleaner version. Also a lifesaver for condensing long readings into something I can actually skim before class. Fair warning - it rewrites but doesn't explain, so use it on your own draft, not to pretend you read the chapter.

ChatGPT (Writing Workflows)

Freemium
Outlines & first drafts

The way I actually use ChatGPT for essays: not to write them, but to argue with it. I ask for thesis options, outline structures, counterarguments to my own points - then I write the paper myself. Most schools now ask you to disclose AI use, so check your syllabus first. Treat it as the friend who brainstorms with you, not the one who writes your essay.

LanguageTool

Freemium
Grammar check without Grammarly's nagging

LanguageTool is what I recommend to every ESL friend who finds Grammarly's suggestions confusing. It supports 30+ languages, so my roommate checks her Spanish essays and I check my English papers in the same tool. The free version catches spelling, basic grammar and punctuation. It nags less than Grammarly, which I mean as a compliment.

Hemingway Editor

Free
Cutting bloated sentences

Paste your essay into Hemingway and watch it light up like a crime scene - yellow for rambling sentences, red for impossible ones, purple for filler words. My professor said my writing 'improved dramatically' the semester I ran every draft through it. It doesn't check grammar; it checks whether a tired reader can survive your prose.

ProWritingAid

Freemium
Deep-dive essay analysis

ProWritingAid is Grammarly for people who want a lecture, not a correction. Its 20+ reports told me my essay was 40% adverbs and that I started six paragraphs with 'However.' The free version only checks 500 words at a time, so I edit section by section - annoying, but it forces me to actually read the feedback.

Scribbr Citation Generator

Free
APA, MLA, Chicago citations

I finished a 15-source bibliography in ten minutes with Scribbr - paste a URL or DOI, pick your style, copy the citation. No account, no paywall, no 'upgrade to export.' It was the difference between me sleeping before a deadline and manually formatting MLA hanging indents at 4am. My entire friend group switched after I showed them.

Research & References

Find sources, cite properly, don't get called out for hallucinations

View all 8 tools →

Perplexity AI

Freemium
Cited research

The research AI that actually shows its work. Every answer has clickable sources, which saved my citations countless times - I can see where a claim came from and decide if it's credible. Fastest way I've found to start an essay: ask Perplexity, check the sources, then dig into the good ones. Pro is free with a student email when the offer is running.

Consensus

Freemium
Evidence-based claims

When I need to settle 'what does the research actually say?' Consensus is my answer. You ask a yes/no question and it pulls real peer-reviewed papers, showing you the evidence behind each side. For argumentative essays this is gold - I stopped skimming 40 abstracts and started actually finding sources that support my claims.

Elicit

Freemium
Literature reviews

Built for the pain of literature reviews: it reads papers and extracts the sample size, methods, and findings into a comparison table. For a 5-page essay it's overkill, but for my capstone lit review it saved me days of work. If you're doing a thesis, this is the tool that makes it bearable.

Semantic Scholar

Free
Finding papers

Free academic search without ads, and the 'cited by' context is the feature I didn't know I needed - it tells you why a paper was cited, not just that it was. The TLDR summaries are shockingly good for deciding whether a paper is worth 20 minutes of my life. I check it before Google Scholar now.

SciSpace

Freemium
Reading dense academic papers

SciSpace lets you highlight any sentence in a paper and get a plain-English explanation instantly - it carried me through my first real literature review. It also explains the math and jargon in the margins. The free tier limits how many papers you can upload per day, but for decoding one brutal paper at a time, it's plenty.

Connected Papers

Freemium
Finding related papers fast

Stuck a seed paper into Connected Papers and got a visual map of its entire research neighborhood - the papers before it, the papers building on it. I used it to fake 'extensive research' for a lit review in one afternoon, except it wasn't faking: the map showed me exactly which papers I actually needed. Five free graphs a month is tight but workable.

Zotero

Free
Managing sources for big papers

Zotero saved my thesis. One click in the browser saves the paper, the PDF, and all the citation info, then generates the entire bibliography in any style. I stopped keeping a spreadsheet of sources like a caveman. It's free forever, no premium tier ambushing you at export time. The Word plugin for inserting citations is clunky but lifesaving.

Mendeley

Free
PDF reading + notes in one place

My lab uses Mendeley because the PDF reader is genuinely nice - highlight, sticky note, and it all syncs to the cloud for free. Groups let the whole team share papers in one library. I prefer Zotero for citations, but if your week is mostly reading and annotating PDFs rather than formatting bibliographies, Mendeley feels better.

Notes & Lectures

Turn chaotic lectures into searchable, organized notes

View all 7 tools →

NotebookLM

Free
Exam prep from your notes

The AI that can't hallucinate because it only answers from your own material. I upload lecture PDFs and slides, and every answer comes with a citation to the exact passage. The audio overview is the sleeper hit - it turned my notes into a podcast I listened to on the bus before finals. Free, and somehow still free.

Otter.ai

Freemium
Recording lectures

The reason I stopped writing frantically during lectures. It records and transcribes everything, timestamped and searchable, with an auto-summary. The 300 free minutes covers about ten lectures a month - enough if you use it for the hard classes. I still take notes, but now I actually listen instead of transcribe.

Notion AI

Freemium
All-in-one workspace

Notion Plus is already free with a school email, which made it my everything app - notes, planner, to-do. The AI add-on summarizes lectures and turns my mess of notes into study guides inside the same page. It's the only note app where AI feels built-in instead of bolted on.

Obsidian

Free
Building a knowledge base

Free, lives on my laptop, and the linking system is the thing that made me a convert - I connect econ notes to stats notes and suddenly concepts click. No built-in AI, but I copy chunks into a chatbot for summaries all the time. It looks plain at first, but that's the point - it's yours.

Microsoft OneNote

Free
Free unlimited notebook

OneNote is the free-est app in this whole list - unlimited pages, no feature paywall, syncs everywhere. My whole organic chemistry class lives in one notebook: handwritten structures on my tablet, typed notes from lecture, PDFs of problem sets. The notebook-section-page hierarchy feels dated, but 'it's all free' fixes a lot of sins.

GoodNotes

Paid
Handwritten iPad notes

Every person in my lecture with an iPad and nice notes is using GoodNotes. The handwriting feels like actual pen on paper, the search recognizes my scribbles, and I import the professor's slides right into the page to annotate during class. It's one of the few apps I've paid for as a student and never regretted - and there's a free version now with 3 notebooks.

Logseq

Free
Outlining and backlink thinking

Logseq is what happens when a note app is built around bullet points and backlinks instead of documents. Every lecture note I take links to concepts from other lectures, and by finals week I can click 'entropy' and see everything I've ever written about it. Steep learning curve for the first week, but it changed how I connect ideas across courses.

Study & Flashcards

Active recall and spaced repetition, minus the manual card-making

View all 4 tools →

Knowt

Freemium
Auto flashcards from notes

Built by students who got tired of Quizlet raising prices. Paste in your notes or lecture transcript and it makes flashcards and practice quizzes automatically - and the free tier is actually usable, not a teaser. The week before exams, this is my starting point.

Anki

Free
Serious memorization

The ugly-but-magical flashcard app that med students swear by. Its spaced repetition schedules reviews right before you'd forget something, so you actually remember for finals month, not just finals night. First 20 minutes feel clunky, then it's a permanent part of your routine. Free on desktop and Android; iOS is a one-time $25.

Quizlet

Freemium
Ready-made decks

The fastest way to get flashcards without making them: search your textbook chapter and someone has almost certainly made the set already. Free tier covers the basics - the paid tier adds better testing modes. I default to it the week before an exam, though the shrinking free tier is why I also keep Knowt around.

RemNote

Freemium
Notes + flashcards combined

The notes-to-flashcards pipeline is unreal - I take notes in class and it quietly turns them into spaced-repetition cards I review later. It replaces two apps with one, but the learning curve is real; give it a weekend. For heavy notetakers who also cram with flashcards, it's the smoothest thing I've used.

Math & Science

Step-by-step solutions for calculus, physics and chemistry

View all 6 tools →

Wolfram Alpha

Freemium
Computational accuracy

When the AI chatbot and Wolfram disagree, I trust Wolfram. It's still the gold standard for math, physics, and engineering - exact answers, step-by-step solutions, and real scientific data. I use it to check my homework answers and to understand where I messed up. Some things never get replaced.

Photomath

Freemium
Checking homework

Point your camera at the problem, get a step-by-step solution from arithmetic to calculus. It's saved my homework grade more than once. The trick is using it to find where you went wrong, not to skip thinking - if you just copy answers, you will absolutely pay for it on the exam.

Symbolab

Freemium
Typed equation help

A calculator that shows its work - type or scan an equation and it walks through every step. The calculus coverage is strong and the practice mode is perfect for drilling the night before a quiz. It's been my homework companion since high school and it hasn't failed me yet.

GeoGebra

Free
Visualizing functions

Free and the fastest way to 'graph it' when a professor asks. Drag a coefficient and watch the curve move live - abstract functions finally make sense when you can see them. For calc and stats I open this before I even open the textbook.

Mathway

Freemium
Instant answers across all math

Mathway is the fastest 'just give me the answer' tool - algebra through calculus, stats, chemistry equations. The catch is nasty: you see the answer free, but the steps cost $10/month. Since learning steps is the entire point of homework, I use it to check my work, then use Symbolab (free steps) when I'm stuck on how.

Desmos

Free
Graphing and visual intuition

Desmos made me actually understand what a derivative does - drag a slider, watch the graph morph in real time. It's the free graphing calculator that every math teacher in America seems to use in class. No account needed, runs in any browser, and the scientific calculator got me through exams where physical calculators weren't allowed.

Presentations

Class decks and project pitches in minutes, not all-nighters

View all 4 tools →

Gamma

Freemium
Slides in 5 minutes

The 'PowerPoint takes three hours, Gamma takes five minutes' tool, and honestly it's not an exaggeration. Paste in a doc or a prompt and you get a class-ready deck with layout done. The free 400 credits cover four or five presentations - I save them for the ones actually worth something.

Canva

Freemium
Design control

The safe pick when I need something to look decent fast - slides, posters, infographics, even my resume. Less automatic than Gamma, but you keep full control of the design, and the AI assistant helps when I'm stuck. There's a template for literally everything.

Beautiful.ai

Freemium
Auto-designed slides

Beautiful.ai treats you like you have zero design sense, because I do. Add content and it rearranges everything to stay balanced - no more slides with one sad bullet point floating in a corner. The free trial is limited, but it saved my group project when the design-inclined member dropped the class a week before presenting.

Slidesgo

Freemium
Free slide templates that don't look like 2007

Slidesgo is where I go when the deadline is tomorrow and the deck needs to not look like every other presentation in class. Free templates for Google Slides and PowerPoint, organized by subject - the education section is huge. You do have to leave the attribution slide or sign up, and the free picks rotate monthly, which is mildly annoying.

Free
Bundle · Verified students

The single best thing I signed up for with my .edu email. GitHub Copilot is completely free - normally $10 a month - plus cloud credits, free domains, and a pile of developer tools. If you write any code at all, verify your student status today. I tell every CS friend about this first.

JetBrains Student License

Free
Free subscription · Students + teachers

Namecheap for Education

Free
Free domain · Verified students

Verified students get a free .me domain for a year - I used mine for my portfolio site and it looks way more professional than a free subdomain. Renewal isn't free after year one, so treat it as a starter domain. Cheap .coms too if you're building something real.

GitLab for Education

Free
Free subscription · Students + teachers

Students get the top Ultimate tier free - advanced CI/CD, security scanning, project management features that cost real money in the corporate world. If your course or side project touches DevOps at all, grab it. My group project's pipelines have never looked smoother.

Azure for Students

Free
Cloud credit · Verified students

$100 in Azure credit plus free-for-12-months services, and the killer part: no credit card required. As a broke student that's what sold me. You also get Azure OpenAI access at credit rates, which was genuinely useful for my AI coursework.

AWS Educate

Free
Cloud credit · Students + educators

Free AWS credits plus self-paced cloud labs and a badge system that looks decent on a resume. If your class touches cloud computing at all, sign up - the labs alone are worth it. I used it to learn the basics without touching my own money.

Google Cloud for Students

Cloud credit · Students via faculty

DigitalOcean Student Credits

Cloud credit · GitHub Student Pack members

Part of the GitHub Student Pack - cloud credit to spin up droplets, which are simple VPS servers. If you want to host a side project on a real server without Azure's complexity, this is the friendliest option I've used. Clean dashboard, great docs.

MongoDB Atlas for Students

Cloud credit · Verified students

Notion for Education

Free subscription · Students + teachers

Notion Plus is free with a school email - unlimited uploads and version history for as long as you study. It replaced my planner, notes app, and to-do list with one thing. The template gallery alone is worth the signup.

Perplexity Pro for Students

Free
Free subscription · Verified students

Perplexity runs free Pro for verified students from time to time - same models as paying users, with the cited answers included. I claimed it with my .edu email and it's been my research sidekick since. Check their education page while the offer's active.

Figma for Education

Free subscription · Students + teachers

Figma's professional tier is free for students - unlimited projects, team libraries, and the full plugin ecosystem including AI plugins. Even my non-designer friends end up using it for group project mockups. The industry standard, free while you're in school - take it.

Canva for Education

Free subscription · Teachers + K-12 students

K-12 teachers and students get full Canva Pro free - every premium template, stock image, and AI tool unlocked. College students should check if their school has a campus license. Even without it, the free tier is generous enough for most student work.

Adobe Creative Cloud Student

Discount · Verified students

Adobe's student plan is roughly 65% off the full Creative Cloud - Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, After Effects. It's not free like the others here, but for design and media students it's the best price you'll ever get on industry-standard tools. I split it with a classmate.

Autodesk Education

Free
Free subscription · Students + educators

Autodesk opens its whole catalog - AutoCAD, Maya, 3ds Max, Fusion 360, Revit - to students for free. For engineering, architecture, and 3D/animation students, this is the single most valuable free perk that exists. Fusion 360 alone costs thousands commercially.

Tableau for Students

Free
Free subscription · Verified students

If you're in any data analytics course, get Tableau Desktop Pro free for a year with a student license - the same license costs real money in the corporate world. Learning it now is a solid resume move if data roles are your target. I put it on my resume the same week.

LinkedIn Learning (via campus)

Free
Free subscription · Students via university

Most universities buy a campus license - check your library portal. The course library is huge and the certificates push straight to your LinkedIn profile, which recruiters actually see. I knocked out a few Excel and public speaking courses between semesters.

Coursera for Campus

Free
Free subscription · Students via university

If your university participates, you get free access to a big catalog of courses and guided projects - Google, IBM, and university courses with real certificates. I took a Google course that actually meant something on my resume. Ask your school if they're enrolled.

O'Reilly Learning (via campus)

Free
Free subscription · Students via university

If your university library subscribes, this is a goldmine - every tech book they've published, video courses, and interactive coding sandboxes. For CS students especially: read the same books the pros use, free. I stopped buying coding books entirely.

DataCamp for Students

Free
Free subscription · Verified students

Verified students get free premium access for a semester - the full catalog of Python, R, SQL, and data science courses with in-browser coding. If you want data skills on your resume without paying for a bootcamp, this is the most hands-on option here.

Apple Education Pricing

Discount
Discount · Verified students + parents + teachers

Verified students get discounted Mac, iPad, and accessories - usually $100-200 off. If you were buying a Mac anyway, it's free money. The Pro Apps Bundle (Final Cut, Logic) is also deeply discounted, which is what sold me.

Spotify Premium Student

Discount
Discount · Verified students

Half price plus Hulu (with ads) and SHOWTIME bundled in - genuinely the best streaming value a student can get. You re-verify yearly and it caps at four years, but for ad-free music while studying it can't be beaten. I signed up the day I got my .edu email.

YouTube Premium Student

Discount
Discount · Verified students

About 40% off regular price, removes all ads, enables background play, and throws in YouTube Music. For anyone who learns from YouTube - which is most of us - the ad-free plus background play combo during study sessions is worth the few dollars a month.

Amazon Prime Student

Discount
Discount · Verified students

Six months of Prime completely free, then half price for up to four years. The free two-day shipping alone justified it for my textbook orders, and Prime Video is a legit bonus. The trial is the longest free window of any perk here - claim it early in the semester.

UNiDAYS

Free
Bundle · Verified students

Not one perk - it's the key that unlocks hundreds of student discounts across Apple, Nike, Adidas, Samsung, and more. Sign up once with your .edu email and you're verified across most student-discount programs on the web. I've saved more than I can count on clothes alone.

Student Beans

Free
Bundle · Verified students

UNiDAYS' main competitor - another free aggregator of student discounts at hundreds of brands. The two overlap but each has exclusive partners, so it's worth having both. Student Beans is particularly strong in the UK and EU.

Free Learning Resources

Open courses, free textbooks and study tools that cost nothing

View all 10 tools →

Khan Academy

Free
Foundational subjects & catch-up

Khan Academy is the first place I send any student who says 'I don't get this.' The lessons are broken into 5-15 minute videos with practice problems right after, so you can actually finish a topic in one sitting. Used it to claw back a C in stats the semester I stopped attending lecture.

MIT OpenCourseWare

Free
Advanced learners & self-study

MIT OpenCourseWare hands you the actual lecture notes, problem sets and exams from real MIT classes - for free, with no signup. It is the single best free resource on the internet if you are self-teaching a hard subject. Not for the faint of heart though: the problem sets are brutal.

OpenStax

Free
Free college textbooks

OpenStax publishes free, peer-reviewed textbooks used at hundreds of US colleges - biology, psych, econ, calc, the usual gen-ed lineup. Saved me around $400 a semester when I switched from the campus bookstore. The PDFs are clean and the online reader works fine on a phone.

Project Gutenberg

Free
Free literature & reading

Project Gutenberg is a 70,000-book library of public-domain works, free in any format you want - Kindle, EPUB, plain text. Lifesaver for English lit classes where the assigned edition costs $25. Skews heavily toward older works, so don't expect to find modern novels here.

Quizlet

Freemium
Exam prep & quick memorization

Quizlet is the fastest way to get flashcards when you don't want to build your own - search 'AP Biology chapter 7' and there's a ready-made set 90% of the time. Free tier covers most study modes, the paid tier adds diagrams and better testing. I default to this the week before an exam.

Anki

Free
Long-term memorization

Anki is the flashcard app med school and language students swear by because its spaced-repetition algorithm actually works - you remember things months later, not just the night before the test. Steep learning curve for the first 20 minutes, then it is permanently on your toolbar. Desktop and Android are free; iOS costs a one-time $25.

Google Scholar

Free
Academic paper search

Google Scholar is the search engine every college paper actually starts from. It indexes peer-reviewed papers, theses, books and abstracts, and links to free full-text versions when they exist. Saved me dozens of times on lit reviews - paste a citation into 'Cited by' to find the newer papers that reference it.

Coursera

Freemium
Real university courses, free

The move nobody tells you: hit 'Audit the course' and you get the full lectures and readings from Yale, Stanford, Michigan - free. No certificate, but the knowledge is identical. I audited an entire machine learning specialization the summer before taking the actual class and walked in already knowing the first month's material.

edX

Freemium
Harvard & MIT courses, free

edX is where I took Harvard's CS50 without paying a cent or leaving my bed - full lectures, problem sets with automated graders, and a real final project. The audit track is free forever (just no certificate). CS50 alone justifies this whole list; it's the best-taught course I've ever experienced, and it costs nothing.

freeCodeCamp

Free
Learning to code, zero cost

freeCodeCamp took me from 'what is HTML' to shipping a real web project without me spending a dollar - the entire platform is a nonprofit with no premium tier lurking. The certifications (responsive web design, JavaScript, Python) are respected enough to put on a student resume. Their 4-hour YouTube deep dives carried my database class.

Study & Productivity

Focus timers, planners and blockers that get you to actually study

View all 9 tools →

Forest

Paid
Phone addicts who need to focus

Forest weaponizes guilt: start a 25-minute study session and a tree grows - leave the app to check Instagram and it dies, and your forest graveyard shows every failure. It sounds silly until you have a dead-tree field reminding you of the four hours you spent on TikTok. The Chrome extension that blocks sites while trees grow is the real killer feature.

Pomofocus

Free
Pomodoro timer with a task list

Pomofocus is a free Pomodoro timer that actually tracks what you accomplished each session, so my 'study log' compiles itself. I use the standard 25/5 cycle for problem sets and 50/10 for essay writing. No account needed, no app download, and the end-of-day report of how many hours were real work versus 'open textbook and vibe' is humbling.

Todoist

Freemium
Managing every deadline in one place

Every assignment, club event and 'email professor back' lives in Todoist. Natural language input is the killer feature: type 'econ essay Friday 5pm' and it schedules itself. The free tier handles 5 active projects, which covered my entire semester. During finals week the 'today' view is the only reason nothing slipped through the cracks.

Google Calendar

Free
Class schedule + time blocking

Boring pick, but the honest truth: every fancy planner app I tried died within a month and Google Calendar survived. All my classes are color-coded blocks, assignment due dates get their own calendar, and time-blocking study sessions is the only reason my 'free Tuesday' didn't evaporate into YouTube. Sharing calendars for group projects takes 10 seconds.

Trello

Freemium
Group projects and thesis tracking

Every group project I've run on Trello actually finished on time. Kanban board with 'To Do / Doing / Done' columns means nobody can pretend they're working - the card sits there, unupdated, in public. Free tier gives unlimited cards, and the Butler automation moved my cards and set due dates automatically. For solo study, it's overkill; for teams, it's magic.

Cold Turkey Blocker

Freemium
Blocking distractions for real

Cold Turkey is the only blocker I couldn't cheat. Block Reddit, YouTube and games until Friday 6pm, and even restarting your computer doesn't undo it - I tried, during a moment of weakness, and the timer just kept going. The Pro version (one-time $39) allows wild features like locking blocks until a certain date. My GPA's real MVP during finals.

Miro

Freemium
Group brainstorming and diagrams

Miro is the infinite whiteboard my group projects live on. Everyone's cursors floating around the same board at once, sticky notes everywhere, mind maps branching out - our entire marketing project plan fit on one board. The free tier's 3 boards is the annoying limit, so we delete old boards ruthlessly. For diagram-heavy classes, it also beats drawing on paper.

Focusmate

Freemium
Body doubling with strangers

Focusmate pairs you with a random stranger for a 50-minute silent video call where you both just... work. Sounds weird, is weird, works anyway - I can't open Reddit when a real human can see my screen. Three free sessions a week is enough for my worst procrastination days. It's the closest thing online to studying in a full library.

TickTick

Freemium
To-do list + Pomodoro in one app

TickTick is Todoist and a Pomodoro timer had a baby, and the baby also does habit tracking. One app holds my assignments, focus sessions, and 'drink water' streaks. The free tier covers the essentials - the premium calendar view is what it gates. If juggling two separate apps for tasks and timers sounds like chaos, this is your fix.

Language Learning

Free ways to actually get fluent, not just streak-obsessed

View all 7 tools →

Duolingo

Freemium
Daily habit + survival basics

The owl works. 200 days of Duolingo got me through tourist-level Spanish and a genuine daily habit - the streak system is manipulative and I love it. But here's the honest ceiling: it's a vocabulary game, not fluency. My speaking still fell apart at an actual restaurant in Madrid. Use it as your daily vitamin, then get real input elsewhere.

LingQ

Freemium
Learning from real content

LingQ flipped language learning for me: instead of textbook dialogues, I import actual news articles and Netflix scripts, and the app tracks every word I know and every word I'm still fuzzy on. Seeing 'the 500 words you should know next' laid out is addicting. The interface is dated, but the method - learning language through content you'd actually consume - is the real deal.

Language Reactor

Freemium
Learning by binge-watching Netflix

Language Reactor adds dual subtitles to Netflix and YouTube, lets you click any word for an instant translation, and auto-pauses after every line. My 'French study' became rewatching Lupin - except after a month I genuinely understood sentences without reading. It's the closest thing to the 'learn through immersion' dream while lying in bed.

HelloTalk

Freemium
Texting native speakers

HelloTalk is a social network where everyone corrects each other's language - I text native speakers in Spanish, they fix my mistakes, I fix their English, everyone wins. The built-in correction tools make fixing someone's message weirdly satisfying. Fair warning: like any social app, some people are there to flirt, but the moments feature (share a photo, get corrections) is genuinely great.

Memrise

Freemium
Hearing how people actually talk

Memrise's secret weapon is video of actual locals saying phrases - not voice actors, real people at real speed with real slang. After a month, I stopped freezing when French speakers talked fast because I'd been training my ear on authentic clips. The free courses are solid; the official Memrise-made ones push the subscription harder.

Busuu

Freemium
Feedback from native speakers

Busuu's killer feature: submit a speaking or writing exercise and actual native speakers correct it within hours. A retired French teacher fixed my pronunciation in detail no app could. The structured courses are more grounded in grammar than Duolingo's. Free tier allows a few exercises per language, so I rotate corrections across my weaker skills.

Forvo

Free
Hearing any word pronounced

Forvo is the pronunciation dictionary - millions of words recorded by actual native speakers. When my Spanish textbook swore 'll' sounds one way but three different people on YouTube said another, Forvo settled it with a dozen recordings from different countries. Free, no account needed for listening. My accent improved just from drilling the words I always butchered.

FlowCV

Free
A free resume that looks custom-designed

FlowCV is the rare resume builder where 'free' actually means free - every template, every export to PDF, no watermark, no 'unlock $9.99' ambush on the download button. My resume looked like a designer made it, and I got two internship interviews with it. The only upsell is their website builder, which you can just... not click.

Rezi

Freemium
AI-tuned resumes for job descriptions

Rezi's AI grades your resume against a specific job posting and tells you exactly what's missing - keywords, format, weak bullet points. I pasted an internship listing, rewrote my bullets per its suggestions, and watched the score climb from 40 to 80. Free tier limits how much AI you get per month, but one good optimization cycle fits in it.

Novoresume

Freemium
Guided resume writing with content tips

Novoresume doesn't just hand you a template - it teaches while you write, with inline tips like 'use numbers, not duties' that genuinely improved my bullet points. The free one-page templates are clean and ATS-safe. It's the training wheels option: best if you've never written a resume and don't have a career center appointment until next week.

Overleaf

Freemium
LaTeX resumes and academic writing

If you're in CS, math, or any field where people worship LaTeX, Overleaf is non-negotiable - my one-page LaTeX resume, built from a template in an afternoon, looks sharper than anything I could drag-and-drop. It also became my tool for problem set writeups and theses. Free tier is enough; the GitHub-style version history and real-time collaboration sealed it for group papers.

Kickresume

Freemium
AI first draft + matching cover letters

Kickresume's AI wrote me a shockingly decent first draft of a cover letter from just the job posting - I spent longer editing it than I would have staring at a blank page. The resume and cover letter templates match visually, which made my application look intentional. Free tier only has 4 templates, but they're the good ones.

Handshake

Free
Internships that actually want students

Every recruiter on Handshake is explicitly looking for students from my school - that's the whole point of the platform. No more LinkedIn ghost-town where internships demand 3 years of experience. Career fair info, on-campus jobs and internship postings all filter to my university. It's how I found the on-campus job paying my coffee habit.

Teal

Freemium
Tracking 30 applications without chaos

Applying to internships turns ugly fast - 27 browser tabs, a spreadsheet from hell, no memory of which cover letter went where. Teal's job tracker keeps every posting, deadline, and resume version in one board, and its matching tool tells me which keywords each posting wants. Free tier covers tracking; the AI resume tailoring is the upsell.

Frequently Asked Questions

What students ask us most

What is the best AI tool for students?

ChatGPT is the most versatile all-rounder, but the best pick depends on the task: Claude writes more naturally, Perplexity is better for cited research, and NotebookLM is unbeatable for summarizing your own lecture notes. Every tool on this site is tested on real coursework.

Is ChatGPT free for students?

Yes, the free tier covers most student needs - explanations, feedback and brainstorming. Heavier usage and the newest models need a paid plan, but some universities offer free ChatGPT Edu access, so check with your IT department first.

How do I get premium software for free as a student?

Most major tools - GitHub Copilot, Notion Plus, Perplexity Pro, JetBrains IDEs - are free with a verified student email. See the Free Student Perks section: 26 premium tools, no trials, all verified in 2026.

Can professors tell if I used AI?

Detection tools are unreliable, but unedited AI text is often obvious - generic phrasing and fake citations. Use AI for outlines, feedback and explanations, then write in your own voice. That is both safer and what these tools are genuinely best at.
