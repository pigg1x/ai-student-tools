AI Assistants

General-purpose AI chatbots for anything college throws at you

View all 3 tools →

ChatGPT

Freemium
Everything

ChatGPT is the first tab I open at 2am before an essay is due. The free tier got me through freshman year - explaining concepts I zoned out on, outlining essays, debugging code that made no sense. One warning I learned the hard way: it will absolutely invent sources if you let it. I never paste its citations into a paper without checking them myself now.

Claude

Freemium
Long readings & essays

The reason I switched from ChatGPT for research papers is the context window - I can drop an entire 200-page PDF in and ask questions like 'what does the author argue in chapter 3?' No more copy-pasting chunks of reading. For essay-heavy majors the free tier is honestly generous enough to be your main AI.

Google Gemini

Freemium
Google Docs users

If your whole academic life runs on Google - Docs, Classroom, Gmail - Gemini just works. It reads your docs without you exporting anything, and it's genuinely good at explaining diagrams and charts from lecture slides. It's not the flashiest AI, but it's the least friction when everything you own is already in Google.

Essay & Writing

Draft, paraphrase, polish and grammar-check your assignments

View all 3 tools →

Grammarly

Freemium
Final essay polish

I installed the browser extension freshman week and forgot about it until I wrote my first essay without it. It catches typos, passive voice, and tone issues everywhere - docs, emails, even messages to professors. I only paid for Premium during thesis season, and the student discount makes it like 40% cheaper. Worth every cent for that one month.

QuillBot

Freemium
Paraphrasing

My go-to for the classic problem: I know what I want to say but it comes out clunky. Paste it in, get a cleaner version. Also a lifesaver for condensing long readings into something I can actually skim before class. Fair warning - it rewrites but doesn't explain, so use it on your own draft, not to pretend you read the chapter.

ChatGPT (Writing Workflows)

Freemium
Outlines & first drafts

The way I actually use ChatGPT for essays: not to write them, but to argue with it. I ask for thesis options, outline structures, counterarguments to my own points - then I write the paper myself. Most schools now ask you to disclose AI use, so check your syllabus first. Treat it as the friend who brainstorms with you, not the one who writes your essay.

Research & References

Find sources, cite properly, don't get called out for hallucinations

View all 4 tools →

Perplexity AI

Freemium
Cited research

The research AI that actually shows its work. Every answer has clickable sources, which saved my citations countless times - I can see where a claim came from and decide if it's credible. Fastest way I've found to start an essay: ask Perplexity, check the sources, then dig into the good ones. Pro is free with a student email when the offer is running.

Consensus

Freemium
Evidence-based claims

When I need to settle 'what does the research actually say?' Consensus is my answer. You ask a yes/no question and it pulls real peer-reviewed papers, showing you the evidence behind each side. For argumentative essays this is gold - I stopped skimming 40 abstracts and started actually finding sources that support my claims.

Elicit

Freemium
Literature reviews

Built for the pain of literature reviews: it reads papers and extracts the sample size, methods, and findings into a comparison table. For a 5-page essay it's overkill, but for my capstone lit review it saved me days of work. If you're doing a thesis, this is the tool that makes it bearable.

Semantic Scholar

Free
Finding papers

Free academic search without ads, and the 'cited by' context is the feature I didn't know I needed - it tells you why a paper was cited, not just that it was. The TLDR summaries are shockingly good for deciding whether a paper is worth 20 minutes of my life. I check it before Google Scholar now.

Notes & Lectures

Turn chaotic lectures into searchable, organized notes

View all 4 tools →

NotebookLM

Free
Exam prep from your notes

The AI that can't hallucinate because it only answers from your own material. I upload lecture PDFs and slides, and every answer comes with a citation to the exact passage. The audio overview is the sleeper hit - it turned my notes into a podcast I listened to on the bus before finals. Free, and somehow still free.

Otter.ai

Freemium
Recording lectures

The reason I stopped writing frantically during lectures. It records and transcribes everything, timestamped and searchable, with an auto-summary. The 300 free minutes covers about ten lectures a month - enough if you use it for the hard classes. I still take notes, but now I actually listen instead of transcribe.

Notion AI

Freemium
All-in-one workspace

Notion Plus is already free with a school email, which made it my everything app - notes, planner, to-do. The AI add-on summarizes lectures and turns my mess of notes into study guides inside the same page. It's the only note app where AI feels built-in instead of bolted on.

Obsidian

Free
Building a knowledge base

Free, lives on my laptop, and the linking system is the thing that made me a convert - I connect econ notes to stats notes and suddenly concepts click. No built-in AI, but I copy chunks into a chatbot for summaries all the time. It looks plain at first, but that's the point - it's yours.

Study & Flashcards

Active recall and spaced repetition, minus the manual card-making

View all 4 tools →

Knowt

Freemium
Auto flashcards from notes

Built by students who got tired of Quizlet raising prices. Paste in your notes or lecture transcript and it makes flashcards and practice quizzes automatically - and the free tier is actually usable, not a teaser. The week before exams, this is my starting point.

Anki

Free
Serious memorization

The ugly-but-magical flashcard app that med students swear by. Its spaced repetition schedules reviews right before you'd forget something, so you actually remember for finals month, not just finals night. First 20 minutes feel clunky, then it's a permanent part of your routine. Free on desktop and Android; iOS is a one-time $25.

Quizlet

Freemium
Ready-made decks

The fastest way to get flashcards without making them: search your textbook chapter and someone has almost certainly made the set already. Free tier covers the basics - the paid tier adds better testing modes. I default to it the week before an exam, though the shrinking free tier is why I also keep Knowt around.

RemNote

Freemium
Notes + flashcards combined

The notes-to-flashcards pipeline is unreal - I take notes in class and it quietly turns them into spaced-repetition cards I review later. It replaces two apps with one, but the learning curve is real; give it a weekend. For heavy notetakers who also cram with flashcards, it's the smoothest thing I've used.

Math & Science

Step-by-step solutions for calculus, physics and chemistry

View all 4 tools →

Wolfram Alpha

Freemium
Computational accuracy

When the AI chatbot and Wolfram disagree, I trust Wolfram. It's still the gold standard for math, physics, and engineering - exact answers, step-by-step solutions, and real scientific data. I use it to check my homework answers and to understand where I messed up. Some things never get replaced.

Photomath

Freemium
Checking homework

Point your camera at the problem, get a step-by-step solution from arithmetic to calculus. It's saved my homework grade more than once. The trick is using it to find where you went wrong, not to skip thinking - if you just copy answers, you will absolutely pay for it on the exam.

Symbolab

Freemium
Typed equation help

A calculator that shows its work - type or scan an equation and it walks through every step. The calculus coverage is strong and the practice mode is perfect for drilling the night before a quiz. It's been my homework companion since high school and it hasn't failed me yet.

GeoGebra

Free
Visualizing functions

Free and the fastest way to 'graph it' when a professor asks. Drag a coefficient and watch the curve move live - abstract functions finally make sense when you can see them. For calc and stats I open this before I even open the textbook.

Presentations

Class decks and project pitches in minutes, not all-nighters

View all 2 tools →

Gamma

Freemium
Slides in 5 minutes

The 'PowerPoint takes three hours, Gamma takes five minutes' tool, and honestly it's not an exaggeration. Paste in a doc or a prompt and you get a class-ready deck with layout done. The free 400 credits cover four or five presentations - I save them for the ones actually worth something.

Canva

Freemium
Design control

The safe pick when I need something to look decent fast - slides, posters, infographics, even my resume. Less automatic than Gamma, but you keep full control of the design, and the AI assistant helps when I'm stuck. There's a template for literally everything.

Free Learning Resources

Open courses, free textbooks and study tools that cost nothing

View all 7 tools →

Khan Academy

Free
Foundational subjects & catch-up

Khan Academy is the first place I send any student who says 'I don't get this.' The lessons are broken into 5-15 minute videos with practice problems right after, so you can actually finish a topic in one sitting. Used it to claw back a C in stats the semester I stopped attending lecture.

MIT OpenCourseWare

Free
Advanced learners & self-study

MIT OpenCourseWare hands you the actual lecture notes, problem sets and exams from real MIT classes - for free, with no signup. It is the single best free resource on the internet if you are self-teaching a hard subject. Not for the faint of heart though: the problem sets are brutal.

OpenStax

Free
Free college textbooks

OpenStax publishes free, peer-reviewed textbooks used at hundreds of US colleges - biology, psych, econ, calc, the usual gen-ed lineup. Saved me around $400 a semester when I switched from the campus bookstore. The PDFs are clean and the online reader works fine on a phone.

Project Gutenberg

Free
Free literature & reading

Project Gutenberg is a 70,000-book library of public-domain works, free in any format you want - Kindle, EPUB, plain text. Lifesaver for English lit classes where the assigned edition costs $25. Skews heavily toward older works, so don't expect to find modern novels here.

Quizlet

Freemium
Exam prep & quick memorization

Quizlet is the fastest way to get flashcards when you don't want to build your own - search 'AP Biology chapter 7' and there's a ready-made set 90% of the time. Free tier covers most study modes, the paid tier adds diagrams and better testing. I default to this the week before an exam.

Anki

Free
Long-term memorization

Anki is the flashcard app med school and language students swear by because its spaced-repetition algorithm actually works - you remember things months later, not just the night before the test. Steep learning curve for the first 20 minutes, then it is permanently on your toolbar. Desktop and Android are free; iOS costs a one-time $25.

Google Scholar

Free
Academic paper search

Google Scholar is the search engine every college paper actually starts from. It indexes peer-reviewed papers, theses, books and abstracts, and links to free full-text versions when they exist. Saved me dozens of times on lit reviews - paste a citation into 'Cited by' to find the newer papers that reference it.
