10 Best Free Learning Resources for Students (2026)

Open courses, free textbooks and study tools that cost nothing

Khan Academy

Free
Foundational subjects & catch-up

Khan Academy is the first place I send any student who says 'I don't get this.' The lessons are broken into 5-15 minute videos with practice problems right after, so you can actually finish a topic in one sitting. Used it to claw back a C in stats the semester I stopped attending lecture.

MIT OpenCourseWare

Free
Advanced learners & self-study

MIT OpenCourseWare hands you the actual lecture notes, problem sets and exams from real MIT classes - for free, with no signup. It is the single best free resource on the internet if you are self-teaching a hard subject. Not for the faint of heart though: the problem sets are brutal.

OpenStax

Free
Free college textbooks

OpenStax publishes free, peer-reviewed textbooks used at hundreds of US colleges - biology, psych, econ, calc, the usual gen-ed lineup. Saved me around $400 a semester when I switched from the campus bookstore. The PDFs are clean and the online reader works fine on a phone.

Project Gutenberg

Free
Free literature & reading

Project Gutenberg is a 70,000-book library of public-domain works, free in any format you want - Kindle, EPUB, plain text. Lifesaver for English lit classes where the assigned edition costs $25. Skews heavily toward older works, so don't expect to find modern novels here.

Quizlet

Freemium
Exam prep & quick memorization

Quizlet is the fastest way to get flashcards when you don't want to build your own - search 'AP Biology chapter 7' and there's a ready-made set 90% of the time. Free tier covers most study modes, the paid tier adds diagrams and better testing. I default to this the week before an exam.

Anki

Free
Long-term memorization

Anki is the flashcard app med school and language students swear by because its spaced-repetition algorithm actually works - you remember things months later, not just the night before the test. Steep learning curve for the first 20 minutes, then it is permanently on your toolbar. Desktop and Android are free; iOS costs a one-time $25.

Google Scholar

Free
Academic paper search

Google Scholar is the search engine every college paper actually starts from. It indexes peer-reviewed papers, theses, books and abstracts, and links to free full-text versions when they exist. Saved me dozens of times on lit reviews - paste a citation into 'Cited by' to find the newer papers that reference it.

Coursera

Freemium
Real university courses, free

The move nobody tells you: hit 'Audit the course' and you get the full lectures and readings from Yale, Stanford, Michigan - free. No certificate, but the knowledge is identical. I audited an entire machine learning specialization the summer before taking the actual class and walked in already knowing the first month's material.

edX

Freemium
Harvard & MIT courses, free

edX is where I took Harvard's CS50 without paying a cent or leaving my bed - full lectures, problem sets with automated graders, and a real final project. The audit track is free forever (just no certificate). CS50 alone justifies this whole list; it's the best-taught course I've ever experienced, and it costs nothing.

freeCodeCamp

Free
Learning to code, zero cost

freeCodeCamp took me from 'what is HTML' to shipping a real web project without me spending a dollar - the entire platform is a nonprofit with no premium tier lurking. The certifications (responsive web design, JavaScript, Python) are respected enough to put on a student resume. Their 4-hour YouTube deep dives carried my database class.
